Print Methods Explained: What Is Actually on Your T-Shirt

Brands rarely put the print method in the product title, but it is the biggest single factor in how a printed tee ages. These are the methods you will meet in India, from most to least common.

Screen printing

Ink is pushed through a fine mesh stencil, one screen per colour. It is the classic method for screen printed t shirts and still the most durable for bold designs with a few flat colours. Plastisol ink sits on top of the fabric and gives bright, opaque colour; water-based ink soaks into the cotton and feels softer, with a slightly faded look. The {{P:ravivar-regular-fit-t-shirt|RRC Club T-Shirt}} is screen printed in red and black on white cotton.

{{IMG:ravivar-regular-fit-t-shirt#1|The screen printed race quote on the back of the}}

DTG (direct to garment)

DTG printing works like an inkjet printer for fabric. The ink goes straight into the cotton, so it handles photographs, gradients and fine detail that would need too many screens. It feels soft from day one. The catch: DTG fades faster than screen printing, especially on dark garments, and it only works properly on high-cotton fabric.

DTF (direct to film)

The design is printed onto a film, coated with an adhesive powder and heat-pressed onto the tee. DTF gives vivid colour on almost any fabric and is popular with print-on-demand sellers. It sits on top of the fabric like a thin skin, so it can feel slightly plasticky, and a poorly pressed DTF print is the one most likely to peel at the edges.

Puff print

A puff print uses an additive that expands under heat, so the design rises off the fabric with a soft 3D texture. It looks great on oversized streetwear tees and logos. It is also more delicate: keep it away from the iron and the dryer.

Water-based inks

Not a separate method so much as an ink choice, usually with screen printing. Water-based inks dye the fibres rather than coating them, which is why the print breathes and moves with the fabric. MotorBae uses water-based, toxin-free inks on the {{P:camaro-z-28-hoodie|Camaro Z/28 hoodie}}.

Embroidery

Thread stitched into the fabric. An embroidered t shirt outlasts any ink because there is nothing to crack or fade, but embroidery adds weight and can pucker on thin fabric, which is why it works best on heavier tees. How to Check a Print Before You Buy

You cannot stretch a tee through a phone screen, but you can still learn a lot from the listing and from the first few minutes after delivery.

  • Look for the fabric weight. A heavy print on a thin 140 to 160 GSM tee twists and cracks sooner. For graphic tees, 180 GSM is a sensible minimum and 240 GSM and above holds a large back print best.
  • Zoom into the product photos. A good print has clean edges with no fuzzy halo. A shiny, plastic-looking surface on a big design usually means a transfer or a thick plastisol layer.
  • Do the stretch test on delivery. Gently pull the printed area sideways. A good print stretches with the fabric. Hairline cracks on a new tee mean the ink was not cured properly, and they will only get worse.
  • Check the inside. With DTG and water-based prints you may see a faint ghost of the design on the inside. That is normal. Glue marks or a stiff patch behind the print point to a heat transfer.
  • Read the care label. Brands that know their print method tell you how to wash it. Generic listings with no care information are a warning sign.

Why Prints Crack and Fade (and Which Ones Last)

Almost every cracked print comes down to one of four causes:

  • Under-curing. Plastisol and DTF need enough heat to bond. Rushed production leaves the ink brittle.
  • Thin, stretchy fabric. When the fabric stretches more than the ink can, the ink splits. Heavier cotton moves less.
  • Hot dryers and irons. Heat softens and then hardens ink layers over and over until they break.
  • Harsh washing. Hot water and strong detergent strip DTG ink and dull water-based prints faster.

In practice, embroidery lasts longest, a well-cured screen print comes next, and DTG and cheap transfers need the most care. A heavier fabric helps every method. Care: Keep the Print Looking New

Turn the tee inside out, wash cold on a gentle cycle, use a mild detergent, and dry in the shade or on low heat. Never iron directly on the print; flip the tee inside out first. Which print method lasts longest on a t-shirt?

Embroidery lasts longest because there is no ink to crack or fade. Among ink methods, a properly cured screen print is the most durable, followed by DTF. DTG prints are soft and detailed but fade faster, especially on dark tees.

What is the difference between DTG and screen printing?

Screen printing pushes ink through a stencil, one colour at a time, and suits bold designs with a few colours. DTG prints the design directly into the fabric like an inkjet printer, so it handles photos and gradients but is less durable over many washes.

Why does the print on my t-shirt crack?

Usually because the ink was not cured with enough heat, the fabric is thin and stretchy, or the tee has been through a hot dryer or iron. Washing inside out in cold water and never ironing the print slows cracking a lot.

What GSM is best for a printed t-shirt?

Around 180 GSM is a good minimum for a regular fit printed tee. For big back prints and oversized fits, 240 to 260 GSM holds the design flatter and longer. MotorBae lists the GSM on every product page.
